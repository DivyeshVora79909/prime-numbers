Adaptive recursive arithmetic
=============================

The original research asks whether exact reachability labels
formed from products of distinct 129-bit prime IDs can stay compact during
arithmetic. Synthetic inputs now include independently random recursively
signed exponents, varying depth and width, and inputs with genuine sharing.
The only ordinary integers used by the benchmarks are bounded fixtures,
local coordinates, counts and residues. No represented product is expanded.

Canonical arithmetic
--------------------

similarity_kernel.multiply_fast(circuit, a, b) returns exactly the same
pure signed hereditary base-2 marking as Circuit.multiply. It splits a
recursive exponent as H + h, where H consists of its signed digits at
positions >= 64 and h is a bounded signed integer formed from lower digits.
The split is exact. For groups with H and K the algorithm computes H+K
once, convolves bounded offsets h+k and accumulates signed coefficients.
If several group pairs produce the same H+K, their contributions combine.
For unrelated H and K there is another shortcut. Encode the signed high
support as temporary masks over the finite observed coordinates. An
addition can cancel opposite signs by masks. A same-sign collision or
new actual adjacency triggers exact recursive carry reduction. This work
depends on the circuit's occupied coordinates, even when their numerical
positions are towers.

If normalized local offsets have magnitude below 2^62, they are separated
from every high position by a vacancy. Distinct high parts differ by at
least 2^64. Thus their local offsets cannot touch across a group boundary,
and concatenating the signed markings yields the exact canonical result.
The general carry reducer handles the other cases. For sufficiently
dense and bounded ordinary spans, NumPy convolves signed coefficient
arrays of at most 32,768 combined positions using exact int64 arithmetic.
A coefficient's magnitude is at most the shorter span, so the arithmetic
has a wide safety margin. The arrays depend on the capped span, not the
absolute position. Changing a common exponent by a tower-size shift does
not make an array bigger.

quotient_kernel.divide_fast(circuit, N, D, max_terms=256) consumes only
canonical inputs. It generates the exact NAF quotient most-significant
digit first, with either sign of numerator or divisor. Let R be the
absolute residual and let k=floor(log2(R/abs(D))). The symbolic bit
lengths plus a shifted comparison determine k. A positive NAF whose
largest occupied position is k can be at most floor(2^(k+2)/3). Thus
compare 3R to abs(D)*2^(k+2) to choose leading position k or k+1.
Subtract the selected shifted divisor, keep the residual sign, and
repeat. A comparison can stream the descending signed terms and stop
once their leading difference outweighs every remaining tail. No
division step visits a vacant binary position. An exact quotient
takes one iteration per emitted term. Iteration work still depends on
the residual and divisor DAGs. A smaller nonzero residual than the
divisor proves inexactness. A term-budget exception leaves integrality
undecided, and a power-of-two divisor has a direct shift path.

Whole-graph signed storage
--------------------------

optimize_storage minimizes serialized H2N1 bytes. Its search allows one
definition per reachable integer, at most one signed digit per exponent
in each definition, and any acyclic arrangement. Digits are sorted by
exponent, and rows are numbered in the same depth-first postorder as
Circuit.encode. Arithmetic continues to use canonical, nonadjacent
digits. decode_storage normalizes each stored row as it reads it.

For a row, let c be the canonical digit, k the incoming carry, and d
the proposed digit at one position. The outgoing carry is (c+k-d)/2.
If c+k is even, d must be zero. If it is odd, either sign is valid.
The search skips vacant positions when the carry is zero. Continuing a
carry through vacant positions consumes one digit at each step, so a
finite term budget bounds the enumeration even at tower-size positions.

The search assigns rows jointly. An exponent used by multiple rows
requires just one definition. A nonzero row always needs its lowest
canonical exponent because that position is fixed by divisibility by
two. The lower bound charges each required row, its minimum signed
digit count, and unavoidable multi-byte reference indices. Complete
graphs are scored by serializing them. The incumbent gives a finite
ceiling on rows and digits. If every cheaper branch is ruled out, that
incumbent is byte-optimal within the format described above.

For a root with many ordinary exponent positions, a bounded dynamic
program can prove a byte minimum without enumerating whole graphs.
It groups possible sets of small exponent nodes by their first missing
node. An optimistic cost can rule out an entire group. It charges
each of those definitions once, and each higher root position for
its edge and at least the cost of defining that position. A carry
recurrence finds the cheapest signed root allowed by those costs.
The bound allows extra sharing and omits some overhead, so an actual
encoding attaining the bound certifies a minimum. All reachable
indices from 64 through the last non-root index need at least one
multi-byte reference. If equality would assign a repeatedly used node
a late index, checking the possible depth-first prefixes can raise
the bound by another byte. The shortcut uses bounded digit columns
through position 8,192, with a density check for wide inputs. Each
tested group charges one state. The wire-layout heuristic tries using
frequently referenced exponent nodes earlier. It scores changes using
the first 64 row IDs and checks the winning candidate's actual bytes.

When every improving graph must use one-byte references, its size is
additive in its rows and edges. The solver processes exponent values
in descending order and remembers the cheapest prefix for each set of
remaining definitions. A carry dynamic program bounds each row's
remaining cost. Sparse inputs can skip entire gaps, including gaps
whose lengths are represented recursively. Outside these cases the
general joint search can still have combinatorial growth.

For the example factors 943433720027638044163815075978910058741 and
705383844795881156131636713278929157563 the respective minima are 233
and 213 bytes. Their canonical product uses 531 bytes. Its smallest
H2N1 graph uses 496. The root relaxation permits 495 only when every
small node from 0 through 8 exists. In a graph attaining that bound,
node 8 would have at least 13 incoming edges and at least 66 nodes
would be numbered ahead of it. That conflicts with the one-byte cost
assumed for all but one of its references. An actual 496-byte encoding
attains the resulting lower bound.

For example, 40 = 2^3 + 2^5 can use 3 = 2^1 + 1 and
5 = 2^3 - 2^1 - 1. Expanding 5 to three terms allows the graph to
share the existing 3 and dispense with the node for 2. The whole
encoding drops from 19 bytes to 18. The same search changes signed
rows inside exponents and at the root.

The default limits the search to 2,048 states and targets a certified
1% gap. The cost per state depends on the input graph and the digit
span. If a completed lower bound is L and the best encoding has U
bytes, the actual excess over the optimum is between 0 and U-L.
The result reports L as lower_bound_bytes and U-L as max_extra_bytes.
Only completed lower bounds contribute. Exhausting the budget returns
the incumbent even when its gap exceeds the target. An unlimited
search defaults to a zero gap and can take combinatorial time.
The proof covers this signed-digit format with its prescribed row
ordering. Adaptive algebraic nodes and packed binary have separate
encoding rules.

Representation search and retained products
-------------------------------------------

Adaptive.view imports the pure signed DAG. Adaptive.pack recursively
visits its exponent subgraphs and considers exact alternatives.

* Pull out the minimum common power. E plus each local offset becomes
  one shared E and one relative pattern.
* Compare blocks by their consecutive exact exponent gaps and signs
  relative to the first sign. Matching signatures give identical blocks
  up to shift and an overall sign. A common block can be stored once
  and multiplied by a signed marking of its translations.
* Check gap-based partitions, whole-pattern periods and selected
  disjoint interior motifs. If translations form an arithmetic
  progression with constant sign, replace them with a geometric node.
  Repeated factors can be folded into a power node.
* Measure the whole reachable DAG in the actual binary format. Keep
  literal encoding as a candidate. Also try mixed literal/rewritten
  child encodings when up to six children differ, since a locally
  smaller child can destroy sharing elsewhere in its parent.

These are exact value rewrites. They select a minimum among the
encodings actually examined, with finite search. rounds tunes the
same-level exploration on new candidates. Every original exponent
depth is traversed bottom-up. An unlimited guarantee of smallest
circuit under an arbitrary rewrite grammar would require a separate
theorem. Even exact partition into the fewest unit-cost disjoint
arithmetic-progression blocks is NP-complete. Doubling the positions
maps that problem to all-positive nonadjacent power supports.
https://eprints.cs.vt.edu/archive/00000162/

Adaptive.multiply defaults to a shared balanced product tree and applies
exact structural rules. For example, G(x,m)G(x^m,n)=G(x,mn). An optional
max_pairs budget tries canonicalization and returns the smaller encoded
candidate. Multiply can therefore preserve provenance for subsequent
updates, or explicitly produce pure markings via multiply_fast. The
former moves canonicalization cost out of the update. It still needs
to process the retained factors during a general residue query.

Adaptive.divide cancels a known stored factor, recognizes translated
copies and geometric factors, attempts a canonical exact quotient, or
keeps a checked quotient gate for an explicit divisor. Within a
geometric node, an exact factor of the term count reduces to a series
at multiplied stride. Shift-only quotients use exponent differences.
A bound can stop canonical quotient search. For an unrelated huge
implicit divisor beyond the bound, the program needs structural
divisibility evidence or an explicit divisor. A supported explicit
divisor is checked with bounded modular arithmetic. Lifted moduli
can grow when quotients accumulate. Large odd moduli on deep towers
may require a caller-supplied, validated period for 2. This does not
affect ordinary exponent positions in the sampled prime workload.

Adaptive.encode emits the shared topological record format H2A1.
Adaptive.decode round-trips data emitted by this kernel and validates
record structure and references. It uses arithmetic facts certified
during construction, so the format is intended for trusted kernel
outputs. Exact rediscovery of arithmetic facts from untrusted records
is a different operation. Signed markings and their adaptive forms
have separate encodings, so within-format ablations isolate motif
search from wire-format differences.

Reading the benchmarks
----------------------

The deep benchmark verifies input and output residues against a separate
oracle that directly finds the powers-of-two residue cycle. Pure old
and new outputs are compared byte for byte. The shared-prefix wide
control has 128 terms per operand. Its randomized local supports share
four high components, so 16 high group sums replace 16,384 independent
recursive additions. The independent wide control has the same root
width and independently generated recursive branches. A motif-only
control injects a repeated signed shape at the root and inside an
exponent to isolate recursive rewrite behavior. Standard random trees
do not acquire an injected motif.

Each quotient benchmark trial loads only numerator and divisor
encodings into a fresh arena. It compares the recovered quotient's
entire canonical encoding with the fixture's quotient. The deep
benchmark also checks canonical quotient recovery on twelve random cases.

The 129-bit ID benchmark tests updates, member and outsider queries,
exact known-factor removal, false removal, and reinsertion. At 8,192
factors the logical product has over one million bits. The tree form
stores factor structure explicitly. Its timings must be interpreted
together with residue time and storage, and compared with the explicit
factor-fold and inferred packed-binary sizes. Packing each prime
individually may make the combined product larger if it loses common
exponent subgraphs shared across IDs.

Open questions
--------------

For pure signed arithmetic use the benchmark's independent wide case as
the stress test. For alternative grammar compression use the motif and
nested controls. For the original reachability application keep the
known-factor update and the outsider query separately timed. Shared
serialization across a collection of labels and sharing-aware rewrite
choices across multiple roots remain unmeasured. The membership delta
still comes from the graph layer when an edge changes.
